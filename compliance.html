Compliance & sustainability

Compliance and sustainability.

Every order passes a compliance check before it is placed. What we verify, the evidence we hold, and what happens when a standard is not met.

The gate

Four checks before factory allocation.

Re-verified annually and checked again at each allocation. A lapsed certificate suspends the unit until it is resolved.

CHECK 01

Valid social audit

A current amfori BSCI, SMETA, WRAP or equivalent report, not expired, and with all critical findings closed.

CHECK 02

Structural & fire safety

RSC-aligned building, fire and electrical inspection with documented remediation progress.

CHECK 03

Environmental controls

Functioning ETP where wet processing exists, chemical inventory, and valid environmental clearance.

CHECK 04

Legal & licensing

Trade licence, fire licence, factory licence, bond licence and BGMEA/BKMEA membership in force.

Context

Responsible sourcing across the network.

The Bangladesh ready-made garment sector employs approximately four million people, the majority of them women. Standards have improved substantially since 2013, and maintaining that progress depends on buyers and buying houses declining to place orders with non-compliant factories.

Pillar one

Social compliance

Workers producing your goods must be of legal working age, paid correctly, able to raise concerns without retaliation, and free to leave their employment. These are conditions of doing business with us, not objectives.

  • No child labour. Age verification at hiring with documented records; no worker under the legal minimum age, without exception.
  • No forced or bonded labour. No retained documents, no recruitment fees paid by workers, no restriction on leaving.
  • Legal wages, paid on time. At or above the Bangladesh minimum wage for the grade, with overtime at the legal premium and digital payment records.
  • Controlled working hours. Overtime voluntary and within legal limits, with one day off in seven.
  • Freedom of association. The right to organise and bargain collectively, without retaliation.
  • No harassment or discrimination. Anti-harassment committees in place and functioning, with a confidential grievance channel.
  • Maternity and childcare rights. Legal maternity leave honoured and on-site childcare where required by law.

Pillar two

Structural, fire & electrical safety

Following the Rana Plaza collapse in 2013, Bangladesh established one of the most intensively inspected factory safety programmes in the garment industry. We place orders only with units covered by that programme.

  • RSC inspection. Structural, fire and electrical inspections under the RMG Sustainability Council, which took over the Accord’s work in Bangladesh.
  • Corrective action plans. We review the CAP status, not just the certificate; an open critical finding blocks allocation.
  • Fire detection and suppression. Alarms, sprinklers or hydrant systems, fire doors, and unobstructed marked exits on every floor.
  • Drills and training. Documented evacuation drills, trained fire wardens and first aiders on each shift.
  • No unauthorised subcontracting. Your order is made where we say it is made. Undeclared subcontracting ends the relationship immediately.

Pillar three

Environment & chemical management

Textile dyeing and washing are water and chemical intensive processes. The following controls are required across the units we work with.

Effluent treatment

A functioning ETP is mandatory for any wet-processing unit, with discharge parameters monitored and records available for inspection.

Chemical management

Chemical inventories maintained against the ZDHC Manufacturing Restricted Substances List, with safety data sheets and controlled storage.

Water reduction

Laser and ozone denim finishing, low-liquor-ratio dyeing and condensate recovery to cut consumption per garment.

Energy & emissions

Rooftop solar, LED conversion, heat recovery on boilers and efficient compressed-air systems across partner units.

Preferred materials

Organic cotton (GOTS), recycled polyester and recycled cotton (GRS/RCS), BCI cotton and responsibly sourced wool on request.

Waste & packaging

Cutting-waste segregation and recycling routes, reduced and recycled-content polybags, and FSC-certified cartons where specified.

Supplier code of conduct

Our supplier code of conduct.

Every factory signs our supplier code of conduct before joining the network. It is binding and includes a right of unannounced access.

Request the full code See our QC standard

ZIM Sourcing Ltd, our buyers and any nominated third-party auditor may enter the factory at any time during operating hours, announced or unannounced, and may speak to workers privately without management present.

All production, including any sub-process such as printing, embroidery, washing or embellishment, must be declared in writing and carried out at approved sites. Undeclared subcontracting is grounds for immediate termination and full financial liability for the order.

Findings are graded critical, major or minor. A critical finding (child labour, forced labour, imminent danger to safety, or denial of access) suspends production immediately. Major and minor findings carry defined corrective windows, both with documented verification and follow-up.

A confidential grievance mechanism must be available to every worker, with the anti-harassment and participation committees legally required in Bangladesh actually meeting and minuting their meetings.

Valid environmental clearance, a functioning ETP where applicable, compliant chemical storage and handling, and honest reporting of consumption data when requested by a buyer.

Where a finding is remediable and the factory is willing to act, we remain engaged and support the corrective process, as immediate withdrawal generally harms workers more than it protects them. Where a factory conceals findings, repeats them or refuses to act, we withdraw and inform the buyer of the reason.
